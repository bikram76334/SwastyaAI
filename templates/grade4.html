Grade4
  • Overview
Description

Commanly Known as Proliferative Diabetic Retinopathy.The most advanced and severe stage. New, fragile, abnormal blood vessels grow along the retina and vitreous humor. These vessels bleed easily, causing vitreous hemorrhages, scar tissue formation, and potential retinal detachment.

Symptoms
  • Sudden floaters or dark spots.
  • Flashes of light
  • Missing areas of vision
  • Sudden or severe vision loss from bleeding or retinal detachment
Causes
  • Neovascularization from VEGF
  • Vitreous hemorrhage and fibrosis
  • Tractional retinal detachment
Affected Parts

Retina, Vitreous humor, Optic disc, Macula

Recommended Checkup
  • Seek immediate specialized medical or surgical treatment (such as Anti-VEGF therapy, laser photocoagulation, or vitrectomy surgery)
  • Schedule frequent, close monitoring with a retina specialist.
  • Avoid sudden physical exertion, heavy lifting, or activities that increase intraocular pressure.
